### 1. Goals with WOOP and a measure
*As anyone growing, I state the 6–18-month outcomes I'm working towards and see whether I'm on track.*
- A goal has a title (the wish), why it matters (the outcome), an obstacle and an if–then plan (WOOP), an identity statement, a deadline and linked growth areas.
- It is measured either by a number (start, target, unit; higher or lower is better) or by up to 5 milestones.
- Logging a value or ticking a milestone updates progress. Progress is compared with where a straight line from start to deadline would be (ahead, on track or behind).
- Goals can be marked achieved or dropped on purpose. At most 3 active goals: focus beats breadth.
- Today shows the active goals compactly and prompts for a measurement when none has been logged for 14 days.
- The Sunday brief gets each goal's progress against expected, and the plan for the obstacle.
- A suggestion comes from the profile (e.g. "Become Head of Product"); nothing is pre-filled with numbers.

### 2. Weekly progress note to one chosen person
*Reporting progress to someone roughly doubles the effect of private monitoring (Harkin et al. 2016).*
- Choose a person from People (a manager, mentor, partner or friend). It defaults to the profile's primary person.
- The note is generated from real data: goals, the Big 3, growth area reps, wins, and one ask for help. It is editable, then copied or shared by the user. ONO never sends anything itself.
- Sending is recorded, so the brief and Today know whether this week's note went out. Today prompts on Fridays.

### 3. Setback protocol
*Self-compassion after failure increases the motivation to improve (Breines & Chen 2012).*
- It is offered on recovery days, after a week under target, or at any time from Me.
- Three short steps:
  1. Treat yourself as you would a friend.
  2. Say what actually happened, without a story.
  3. Name the smallest next step, which becomes today's first priority.
- It is saved as a `setback` log. The brief counts setbacks met with a next step.

### 4. Quarterly review and plan
- Every 12 weeks (or at any time), review wins, lessons and each goal: keep, change or drop.
- Rate the 8 life areas (life wheel); the lowest suggests the next growth area.
- Choose the next quarter's 1–3 goals. Fresh-start prompt at the turn of the quarter.

### 5. Profile editor
- Edit name, roles, organisation, focus, domain, path, key people (relationship, rhythm, primary) and the coach's context.
- Stored in the user's state and overriding the built-in profile; it becomes step 1 of onboarding.
