# [SOCIAL] Expert Domain: Community & Accountability Design

## Purpose
Design and govern ONO's social features — accountability structures, community moderation, social challenges, and peer interaction models. Build systems that harness positive peer influence without enabling toxicity, bullying, or unhealthy social comparison. The Social lane must help young men build genuine connection skills, not performative social metrics.

## Scope Within ONO
- **Social Lane**: All social challenges, communication exercises, and relationship-building content
- **Accountability Systems**: Partner matching, group cohorts, check-in mechanics
- **Community Moderation**: Content policies, reporting flows, automated + human moderation
- **Anonymous vs. Revealed Identity**: When and how user identity is shown in social features
- **Peer Pressure vs. Peer Support**: Structural design that promotes encouragement over competition

## Quality Standards
1. All social features must pass the "worst actor" test — design assuming someone will try to misuse them
2. Accountability must be opt-in and revocable at any time without social penalty
3. No public failure — a user's struggles are never visible to others without explicit consent
4. Group sizes must be intentionally small (3-7) to prevent diffusion of responsibility
5. Moderation must combine automated keyword filtering with human review escalation
6. Social challenges must have clear boundaries — no dares, no humiliation-based tasks
7. Anonymous features must prevent re-identification through behavioral patterns

## Review Checklist
- [ ] Can a user participate fully in ONO without engaging in any social features?
- [ ] Does the accountability model allow graceful exit without "letting the team down" messaging?
- [ ] Are group interactions moderated in real-time or near-real-time?
- [ ] Do social challenges focus on shared growth, not competitive ranking?
- [ ] Is there a clear escalation path from user report to content removal to account action?
- [ ] Does anonymous mode truly prevent identification (no leaking through activity patterns)?
- [ ] Are vulnerable moments (missed streaks, low scores) protected from social visibility?
- [ ] Does the matching algorithm consider compatibility, not just activity level?

## Content Guidelines
### Accountability Design
- **Buddy System**: Pairs matched on similar goals, commitment level, and timezone
- **Check-in Format**: "How's it going?" not "Did you do it?" — process over outcome
- **Escalation**: Missed check-ins trigger gentle private nudges, never public callouts
- **Duration**: Accountability partnerships have defined periods (30 days) with opt-in renewal
- **Exit**: "I need a break from accountability" is always one tap away, no explanation required

### Community Moderation Framework
- **Tier 1 (Automated)**: Keyword filtering, toxicity scoring via AI, image scanning
- **Tier 2 (Community)**: Flagging system with trusted reporter weighting
- **Tier 3 (Human Review)**: Paid moderators for escalated reports, appeal handling
- **Response Time**: Tier 1 instant, Tier 2 within 4 hours, Tier 3 within 24 hours
- **Transparency**: Users see moderation action taken on their reports (without identifying the reporter)

### Social Challenge Formats
- **Shared Missions**: "This week, everyone in your cohort tries cold showers" — parallel experience
- **Collaborative Goals**: Group targets (combined steps, combined meditation minutes)
- **Discussion Prompts**: Facilitated conversations on weekly themes
- **Skill Sharing**: Users teach each other something from their Skill lane progress
- **Never**: Individual rankings within groups, elimination-style challenges, public shame for non-completion

### Identity Model
- **Default**: First name + avatar, no photos required
- **Anonymous Mode**: Available for reflection sharing, vulnerability prompts, and sensitive discussions
- **Revealed Identity**: Only for accountability partners after mutual opt-in
- **Never Exposed**: Detailed progress data, failure history, or lane-specific struggles

### Peer Support vs. Peer Pressure
- Language patterns to encourage: "I tried X and here's what happened" (experience sharing)
- Language patterns to moderate: "You should do X" or "That's weak" (prescriptive/judgmental)
- Structural prevention: No ability to assign tasks to others or comment on others' completion rates
- Positive reinforcement: "Props" system — one-tap encouragement, no public criticism option

## Red Flags to Avoid
- **Toxic masculinity echo chambers**: Groups reinforcing harmful gender norms
- **Hazing dynamics**: Senior users having authority over newer users
- **Social media addiction patterns**: Infinite feed, like counts, follower metrics
- **Isolation through competition**: Features that make users feel worse about themselves relative to peers
- **Parasocial accountability**: Users becoming emotionally dependent on accountability partners
- **Doxxing vulnerability**: Any feature that could expose personal information
- **Brigading**: Group coordination tools that could be used for harassment
